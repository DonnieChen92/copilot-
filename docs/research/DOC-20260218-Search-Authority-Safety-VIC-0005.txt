SECTION 1 — Problem & Scope
================================================================================

Goal:
When searching the web for information related to government, legislation,
property, commercial entities, and community services within Melbourne /
Victoria / Australia (starting from VIC 3000 — City of Melbourne):

1. Assign "authority level + trust weight" to each web source
2. Verify truthfulness for government, legislative, property & business data
3. Include personal/community feedback while avoiding:
   - Directed implantation (导向型植入)
   - Backend manipulation (后台引导误判)
   - Malicious redirects (重定向)
   - Website pollution (网站污染)
4. Ensure technical security of visited websites (HTTPS/cert/domain/redirect)

Principle: Bi-directional inclusion of personal commentary as free speech,
open & transparent, accepting suggestions and criticism — while prohibiting
cyber-bullying (网暴).


SECTION 2 — Authority Source Hierarchy (6 Levels)
================================================================================

Level 1: Government & Legislative Bodies (最高权威)
--------
  Domain ID: .gov.au / .vic.gov.au (policy-restricted, government-only)
  Key VIC / Melbourne sites:
    - vic.gov.au                         — VIC Government unified portal
    - legislation.vic.gov.au             — VIC legislation (original legal texts)
    - melbourne.vic.gov.au               — City of Melbourne (VIC 3000 council)
    - vic.gov.au/know-your-council       — All 79 council info & performance

  Rule: .gov.au / .vic.gov.au with content matching page title, traceable
  to specific department/contact = highest authority + primary source.


Level 2: Official Bodies / Official Organisations
--------
  Government-established or government-authorised professional bodies:
    - abs.gov.au           — ABS (Australian Bureau of Statistics)
    - data.vic.gov.au      — DataVic (VIC open data, 4800+ datasets)
    - land.vic.gov.au      — Land Use Victoria / LANDATA
    - consumer.vic.gov.au  — Consumer Affairs Victoria
    - scamwatch.gov.au     — National Anti-Scam Centre / Scamwatch (ACCC)
    - cyber.gov.au         — Australian Cyber Security Centre

  Rule: Statistics/policy/guidelines from these bodies = "professional +
  authoritative", second only to legislative text itself.


Level 3: Company Official Publications
--------
  For commercial / property entities from VIC 3000 onwards:
    - ABR (Australian Business Register): gov registry for businesses
    - ABN Lookup: public query tool for ABN/ACN/company name

  Verification:
    - Website must show ABN, matchable in ABN Lookup
    - Address in VIC 3000 or corresponding suburb
    - Contact verifiable (phone, physical address on map)

  Rule: ABN present + found in ABR = legitimate company (not necessarily
  trustworthy, but not a "ghost store"). No ABN or ABN not found + claiming
  "Australian local brand" = high alert (ACCC ghost store pattern).


Level 4: Academic Publications
--------
  Domain: .edu.au (Australian universities & research institutions)
  Example: unimelb.edu.au

  Rule: Academic sources for theory + methodology support. Do NOT substitute
  for government guidance or legal text on local policy/regulation/admin.


Level 5: Mainstream Media
--------
  Australian context "mainstream media":
    - ABC News (abc.net.au) — public broadcaster, "most trusted news source"
    - The Age — Melbourne's newspaper of record
    - Herald Sun, The Australian — cross-reference multiple outlets

  Rule: Media = "alert signal" for government actions / regulatory updates.
  Final authority falls back to Consumer Affairs / relevant legislation.
  Always cross-reference multiple media sources to avoid single-stance bias.


Level 6: Community Sources / User Comments
--------
  Usage principle:
    1. Only as "experience feedback signal", NOT as legal/factual basis
    2. For property agents/developers, use:
       - Google Maps / Apple Maps reviews
       - Independent review sites (ProductReview, Trustpilot etc.)
       - Watch for fake positive/negative review flooding
    3. Community opinion = "input" for decisions, but weight clearly
       below levels 1-5

  Cultural principle:
    - 文明评论 (civilised commentary)
    - 禁止网暴 (prohibition of cyber-bullying)
    - Free speech + open transparency + accept suggestions & criticism


SECTION 3 — Quantitative Authority & Truthfulness Scoring Model
================================================================================

Total Score T = 0.4 × Source Authority
              + 0.2 × Transparency
              + 0.2 × Evidence Quality
              + 0.1 × Security
              + 0.1 × Cross-Verification

Each dimension scored 1–5:

3.1 Source Authority (1–5)
  5: .gov.au, .vic.gov.au, parliament, ABS, Land Use VIC, DataVic,
     Consumer Affairs, Cyber.gov.au
  4: .edu.au universities, authoritative research institutions,
     government-authorised semi-official bodies
  3: Large legitimate company websites (ABN + ABR verified), mainstream media
  2: General .org.au, personal blogs, forums (no clear endorsement)
  1: Unknown source, suspicious imitation sites, content farms

3.2 Transparency (1–5)
  Checks:
  - Clear About / Contact / Privacy Policy / Disclaimer
  - Organisation structure, legal entity, ABN disclosed
  - Articles have date, author, data source citations
  - Revision history / correction notices present

3.3 Evidence Quality (1–5)
  - Cites Level 1-2 authoritative data (ABS, DataVic, Land Use VIC etc.)
  - Provides specific methodology, sample size, time range
  - Separates "opinion/speculation" from "fact/data"

3.4 Security (1–5)
  Based on 5-point safety check (see Section 4)

3.5 Cross-Verification (1–5)
  Rule: Any key fact (property regulation, title info, population data,
  subsidy details) must be cross-verified in >=2 different Level 1-2
  authoritative sources.


SECTION 4 — Website Safety Checks (5 Items)
================================================================================

Check A: HTTPS + Valid Certificate
  - URL begins with https://
  - No browser security warnings
  - Certificate CA is legitimate
  - Certificate within validity period
  - Certificate domain matches visited domain
  - ACSC recommends only entering sensitive info on HTTPS sites

  Scoring:
    5: Enforced HTTPS, HSTS, valid certificate
    3-4: Mostly HTTPS, minor HTTP resources
    1-2: Plain HTTP or certificate errors

Check B: Domain Registration Entity
  - .gov.au / .vic.gov.au: strict policy, government-only entities
  - .com.au / .net.au: requires local entity or ABN (higher bar than .com)
  - Verify via WHOIS or auDA query tools

Check C: Official Primary Domain
  - Is this the officially published domain? (e.g. City of Melbourne in
    all official channels points to melbourne.vic.gov.au)
  - Watch for lookalike domains: vic-gov.au.com, gov-au-vic.org = NOT official

Check D: No Redirect to Unknown Third-Party TLD
  - After clicking search result, check for:
    - Multiple redirects
    - Landing on unrelated overseas strange-suffix domains
    - Frequent download/plugin/prize popups
  - ACCC & Scamwatch: scam sites often impersonate local business/government

Check E: Browser Security Alerts
  - Any "This site is not secure" / "Invalid certificate" / "Malware detected"
    = score 0, exit immediately
  - Browser + security tools (Safe Browsing etc.) joint warning = exit


SECTION 5 — Anti-Manipulation & Pollution Detection
================================================================================

5.1 Search Engine Habits
  1. Priority: use site: and suffix constraints
     - Government: site:vic.gov.au, site:*.gov.au
     - Legislation: site:legislation.vic.gov.au
     - Official data: site:data.vic.gov.au + keyword

  2. Exclude ads & content farms
     - Skip results marked "Ad" (especially property, loans, investment)
     - Check domain + org name in result snippet before clicking

  3. Multi-engine cross-reference
     - Use >=2 different search engines (Google + DuckDuckGo/Bing)
     - If "shocking info" only appears on one corner site = likely noise

5.2 Directed Implantation Detection
  Red flags:
  - Extreme single-stance + violent emotional expression, no Level 1-2 refs
  - "Buy now" / "Sign up now" everywhere, info bound to sales content
  - Long article with zero external references/reports/legal links
  - Simple "magic answer" for complex problem, forced toward product/stance

  Rule: Even if technically HTTPS-safe, these pages should be heavily
  downweighted in the authority model.


SECTION 6 — VIC 3000 Property Verification Example
================================================================================

Scenario: Verify property title, zoning & current buy/sell regulations for
an address in VIC 3000.

Step 1 — Title & Ownership:
  LANDATA / Land Use Victoria → Property & Parcel Search → Property Report
  Confirms: LGA, zoning, utilities

Step 2 — Council Information:
  Know Your Council → City of Melbourne → performance reports

Step 3 — Relevant Legislation:
  legislation.vic.gov.au → Property Law Act, Sale of Land Act etc.

Step 4 — Transaction/Regulatory Rules:
  consumer.vic.gov.au → Buying/selling property, underquoting rules,
  consumer rights

Step 5 — Specific Agent/Developer:
  Check official website (.com.au), verify ABN displayed
  ABN Lookup → confirm ABN exists, registered in Australia

Step 6 — Media + Community:
  ABC News, The Age → search project/developer reports
  Google Maps reviews → civilised feedback, watch for review flooding


SECTION 7 — Quick Reference ("Pocket Rules")
================================================================================

For every new website/information, especially regarding policy, law,
property, money:

1. Check domain suffix first:
   .gov.au / .vic.gov.au → trust priority
   .edu.au → academic/theory reference
   .com.au → check ABN first
   Other suffixes → default low weight

2. Verify HTTPS + valid certificate, no strange redirects, no browser warnings

3. Confirm entity identity:
   Government/official → referenced in other official channels?
   Company → findable in ABN Lookup?

4. Ask two questions:
   - Is this info confirmed in >=2 Level 1-2 authoritative sources?
   - Is this page objectively explaining facts, or pushing a stance/product/emotion?

5. When money or personal info involved, follow Scamwatch "Stop-Check-Protect":
   STOP  → Don't rush to pay, don't rush to enter personal info
   CHECK → Check domain, check ABN, check official channels
   PROTECT → If anomaly found, exit & consider reporting to Scamwatch
